# Fool me once

`?scene=fool-me-once` adds a 25.5-second robotic reconstruction of George W.
Bush's familiar verbal stumble. It preserves all twelve songs and the existing
three movie scenes. The user asked for another recognizable, funny speech with
the graphical staging shown in their Jacob Matson press-conference video.

The words come from the official presidential remarks at East Literature Magnet
School in Nashville on 17 September 2002:

- [White House transcript](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2002/09/20020917-7.html)
- [Public Papers of the Presidents](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-2002-book2/pdf/PPP-2002-book2-doc-pg1607.pdf)
- [White House event photograph](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2002/09/images/20020917-7_d091702-515h.html)

This is the short saying, not the full presidential address. The official U.S.
government speech text is public domain. The audio is entirely generated by the
CHIMERA physical airway; there is no recorded presidential voice, news clip or
cloned voice. Phrase timing and intonation are staged by hand. Explicit pauses
separate the false start from the recovery, and the captions follow those same
times. The prepared script holds the OO in the central word, gives its lateral
release less time, and pronounces the emphatic preposition with a full vowel.
These are settings for this performance, not changes to the shared engine.

The black stage uses the head's glowing line renderer: short swept hair, a suit,
red tie, lapel flag, two microphones, a lectern emblem, an American flag and a
repeating event backdrop. The mouth uses the actual voice telemetry. Head turns,
the small shrug and pointing hand use absolute playback time, so they freeze on
pause and return correctly after seeking. The close and wide cameras also share
that clock. The character is deliberately schematic rather than a likeness.

`src/ui/render/pressConference.ts` owns the procedural scene. `press` is a new,
single-speaker scene setting with its own cast and cameras; the other scenes
still require their original main characters. `MovieStage` retains its voice
models until unmount, then releases the models and renderer. Existing scene
video recording, subtitles and audio download also work through this route.

Rebuild the audio and matching mouth telemetry with:

```sh
bun tools/render-fool-me-once.ts
bun run content:manifest
bun run test --timeout 15000
bun run typecheck
bun run build:static --base=/chimera-song-mouth/
```

Native scene tests use the actual prepared speech track to check articulation,
pause, seek, camera framing at desktop and phone sizes, and geometry disposal.
Audio checks cover each utterance, the deliberate silent hesitations, mix
headroom and the full telemetry duration. Native projections of the real scene
geometry were inspected; these are not browser screenshots. Browser playback,
video recording and layout lint were not run because browser access was
previously declined.
