# The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — the discovery of Oz, the Terrible

`?scene=wizard-of-oz`, 3:12. Staged from chapter XV of L. Frank Baum's book (1900, public
domain), cut for length. The words are Baum's, from Project Gutenberg's edition (ebook 55). No
film, recording or later adaptation was used, and nothing here has a source clip: the dialogue is
laid out by the render tool, one line after another, each ending where the engine's plan ends.

## The one voice at two sizes

The great Voice and the little man are the same airway (`body {female:.3, child:.1}`). A Voice
line is planned `size` times faster and higher, rendered, then played back `size` times slower.
Slowing a recording lowers its pitch and every resonance by the same factor, which is what a
throat `size` times larger in every dimension would do. So the Voice is the man, 1.38 times
larger; once Dorothy says the Witch is dead it shrinks to 1.22. Nothing else is done to its
timbre: the mix adds some low end and the hall. The telemetry for those lines is read back on
the scene clock (frame times rewritten, pitch divided by `size`), so one mouth track serves the
whole scene and the little man's mouth can be watched behind the screen from the first line.

Two sentences are spoken at both sizes, "I am Oz, the Great and Terrible": `tests/throne.test.ts`
checks the Voice's is more than an octave lower.

## Clear speech

Every line goes through `clearPhrases` (`src/scene/clarity.ts`) before planning: vowels are held
longer, reduced ones most. The scene is 22 seconds longer for it, and Whisper's word errors on the
mix fell from 16.8 % to 11.2 % by the same line-by-line measure (`tools/scene-clarity.py
wizard-of-oz`, which trims each line's window harder, prints 12.5 %). See [speech-clarity.md](speech-clarity.md). The name "Oz" itself is usually heard as
"I" or "ah"; nothing was done about that.

## Staging

`src/scene/throne.ts` holds the fixed moments (`THRONE_ROAR`, `THRONE_CRASH`, `THRONE_AXE`,
`THRONE_DEMO`) and every pose as a function of the scene clock. The render tool lays the lines
out around those moments and stops with the value to set if an edit moves them.

One liberty: in the book the room is empty while the Voice speaks. Here the great Head from
Dorothy's first visit hangs over the throne on its wire, lit, with a jaw that follows the Voice.
When the screen lands it goes dull and swings; it moves once more when Oz explains the thread.
The Lion's roar is his own airway, driven hard in the mix. The drone, the hall and the screen's
clatter are generated by the tool.

## Rebuilding

    bun tools/render-oz-scene.ts --plan   # the timeline only
    bun tools/render-oz-scene.ts          # about fifteen minutes; needs ffmpeg
    bun run content:manifest

Not bit-reproducible across machines only in the MP3 encoder; the voices and telemetry are.
